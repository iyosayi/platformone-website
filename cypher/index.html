Questions

FAQ

What is Cypher?

Cypher gives organisations visibility and control over how employees use AI. It evaluates AI interactions against your policies and can allow, warn, redact, or block at the moment of interaction.

How does Cypher work?

Cypher sits between employees and the AI tools they use. When an employee interacts with an AI service, Cypher evaluates the interaction against your policies and applies the appropriate outcome—allow, warn, redact, or block. The decision is recorded for review.

Is Cypher just DLP with an AI label?

Not exactly. Traditional DLP focuses on protecting sensitive data across an organisation. Cypher is purpose-built for employee interactions with AI, applying policy at the moment of interaction and giving organisations visibility into how AI is being used.

What can Cypher detect?

Cypher can detect sensitive information based on the detectors and policies configured for your organisation, including identifiers such as NIN, BVN, and other forms of PII. Detection capabilities can be configured to match your organisation’s requirements.

What happens when Cypher detects sensitive information?

The outcome depends on your policy. Cypher can allow the interaction, warn the employee and let them choose whether to continue, redact sensitive information, or block the interaction entirely.

Does Cypher replace our existing security tools?

No. Cypher works alongside your existing security environment. It adds visibility and control around employee AI use without replacing identity, device management, or other security infrastructure.

Can Cypher see every AI interaction in my organisation?

Not every interaction. Cypher’s current focus is browser-based employee interactions with AI. Account type can also be identified where detectable.

What happens to the data Cypher inspects?

Cypher’s data handling, retention, and access controls are defined based on your organisation’s requirements. Your team can establish what is inspected, what is retained or redacted, and who can access decision records before deployment.

What does Monitor do?

Monitor warns an employee when an interaction raises a policy concern and gives them a choice: cancel the interaction or continue.

What gets recorded in a decision record?

Decision records capture the context needed to understand what happened, including the user, AI service, policy triggered, outcome, and time of the decision.
